# On Your Processor v0.6: Below the Kernel Design

## Objective

Expand the twenty-chapter v0.5 manuscript from roughly 24,000 words toward 30,000–35,000 words by adding new jurisdictional collisions rather than repeating the thesis. The expansion gives the SoC and embedded processors stronger comic identities, lets XNU test the practical limit of shutdown authority, and follows the authority question below software into power and infrastructure.

The technical-integrity rule remains unchanged:

> If proven, say it. If inferred, label it. If an undocumented Apple string exists, quote it without inventing semantics.

Fiction may be impossible. It may not impersonate evidence.

## Structural decision

The book becomes twenty-one numbered chapters.

- Chapters 1–17 keep their present subjects and order.
- Chapter 18, **The Hardware Family Dinner**, absorbs the SoC, embedded-microcontroller faction, and strongest fictional-daemon ensemble material.
- Chapter 19, **At the Mercy of the Kernel**, absorbs the Five-Minute Kernel incident.
- A new Chapter 20, **Below the Kernel**, follows authority from execution hardware through power and infrastructure, then allows the argument to escape into explicit satire.
- The current Hyprvisor epilogue becomes Chapter 21 and still ends at `moo.`

The final sequence is therefore:

1. the whole hardware family argues in one room;
2. XNU demonstrates that ending a world is not the same as governing it;
3. the jurisdiction map leaves the motherboard;
4. another kernel discovers that it is somebody else's tenant.

Nothing follows `moo.` in the reading sequence.

## Editorial method: half archive, half reconstruction

The writers-room material is treated as a collection of canon fragments, not as paste-ready manuscript prose.

- Preserve recovered exchanges verbatim when their timing and character voice already work.
- Reconstruct transitions, setups, technical framing, and missing exchanges in the established OYP voice.
- Prefer the original punchline when several versions exist; rewrite only when accuracy, continuity, or page rhythm requires it.
- Do not reproduce conversational praise, planning language, emoji commentary, or explanations of why a joke is funny.
- Every new scene must either introduce a distinct authority boundary or pay off one already established.

## Distributed character expansion

The new characters do not receive a standalone lore chapter. They appear where their claimed authority is already under examination.

### Fiction labels

`amfidd` and `iBootd` are invented OYP characters. Their first appearances must say so plainly in the body text, just as the manuscript labels fake launchd. Their Apple-shaped names must never read as discovered or undocumented system components.

The evidence ledger records both under dramatization identifiers. Dialogue involving them may borrow real vocabulary only when the surrounding prose keeps the vocabulary's real meaning separate from the invented character.

### Parenthood and the boot graph

The parenthood argument should be distributed across the boot and process-family chapters. It contrasts three relationships that ordinary language too easily calls "parent":

- an earlier boot stage verifies or hands off to a later stage;
- a process creates or supervises another process;
- a fictional character claims family resemblance because somebody appended `d` to a name.

The joke is that the family insists these are genealogical relationships. The technical prose must explicitly refuse that simplification. A verified boot handoff is not Unix process parentage, and an invented daemon suffix is not an architectural mechanism.

`iBootd` is SEP's fictional answer to persistence. Actual iBoot should react to "you but persistent" as a personal insult; XNU objects that appending `d` to a boot component does not make it a daemon. The scene preserves the writers-room punchlines where recoverable and supplies new setup around them.

### OIK and AMFI

The OIK/AMFI conflict belongs beside the book's existing trust and enforcement distinctions. Their personal beef dramatizes overlapping involvement without collapsing them into one authority or inventing a private call graph.

The scene should use documented public roles where available, label build-specific observations as observations, and keep any interpersonal motive entirely inside dramatization. The technical lesson is not "which one outranks the other" but which decision, artifact, or enforcement boundary is being discussed at that moment.

### `amfidd`

`amfidd` is cryptographically rigorous and socially vulnerable. The character inherits Gatekeeper's suspicion and launchd's need to know who is responsible, then becomes embarrassingly persuadable when both launchd and SEP claim familiarity with someone.

Protect this emotional sequence:

```text
launchd:
I know him.

amfidd:
noted.

Gatekeeper:
developer cannot be verified.

amfidd:
also noted.

SEP:
I know him too.

amfidd:
okay that's actually pretty compelling.

Gatekeeper:
THAT IS NOT HOW CODE SIGNING WORKS.

launchd:
that's my boy 🥹
```

The genealogy joke is "launchd plus Gatekeeper produced bureaucracy, but raised it in a loving home." It is character canon, not a technical claim about AMFI, Gatekeeper, or a real daemon.

Use **part of me**, not **physically inside me**. This avoids implying a particular die or package arrangement while preserving the family joke.

## Chapter 19: the Five-Minute Kernel

The incident extends the shutdown chapter's existing distinction between power and government. XNU can stop execution on the Application Processor without understanding or governing every independent subsystem affected by that decision.

Protect the provocation:

```text
XNU:
I HAVE TEN CPU CORES.

SEP:
correct.

XNU:
you don't.

SEP:
correct.

XNU:
therefore I win.

SEP:
turn them off then.

XNU:
STOP FUCKING SAYING THAT.
```

The later shutdown ends XNU's sentence when Application Processor execution ceases. SEP gets the last reaction inside the scene:

```text
[Application Processor execution ceased]

SEP:
...

SEP:
bro really turned himself off
```

The passage must distinguish a comic total shutdown from ordinary core offlining, sleep, idle states, and the actual coordinated shutdown path. It must not claim that SEP necessarily remains conversationally active after the exact real-world event dramatized.

Protect the revised thesis line:

> Privilege can end a world without understanding it. That is power, not government. Apple and modern politics still argue about who invented this.

## Chapter 20: Below the Kernel

This chapter asks the jurisdiction question downward without pretending that the resulting chain is a single technical hierarchy.

### Grounded descent

The first half follows real dependencies at a deliberately high level:

```text
XNU
  ↓ execution on
Application Processor
  ↓ integrated into
SoC platform
  ↓ operated through
power-management hardware and firmware
  ↓ supplied by
battery / charger / outlet
  ↓ connected to
electrical infrastructure
```

The prose should say explicitly that these arrows name different relationships. Execution, physical integration, power control, stored energy, electrical supply, infrastructure ownership, regulation, and taxation are not interchangeable forms of authority.

The chapter's visible hinge is:

> The jurisdiction map has now left the motherboard.

The Battery and Charger scene begins the escape:

```text
XNU:
I control execution.

SoC:
on which hardware?

Power Management:
while powered how?

Battery:
using whose energy?

Charger:
whose energy?

Battery:
GET THE FUCK OUT.
```

The text should remain broad enough to avoid inventing a single component called `Power Management` with universal authority. That name is a dramatized ensemble role unless a specific documented component is identified.

### Infrastructure and satire

The outlet, utility, grid, and power plant extend the dependency chain. Government enters as regulator, owner, customer, or legal authority only in appropriately qualified examples; the chapter must not imply that every grid has the same ownership or governmental structure.

The IRS does not appear as a tidy next layer. It interrupts Government's attempt to explain itself. This preserves the joke and prevents the diagram from presenting taxation as a literal component in electrical delivery.

The power plant earns the callback:

```text
Power Plant:
I AM LITERALLY THE POWER PLANT.

SEP:
on your grid.
```

After the serious point lands, the chapter enters a plainly marked metaphysical postscript. Physics, Causality, and Spacetime are satire, not extensions of the technical evidence chain.

Protect the final exchange of Chapter 20:

```text
Power Plant:
Physics.

Physics:
what.

Power Plant:
who authorized the Big Bang.

Physics:
that's not really—

Power Plant:
WHO SIGNED OFF ON
INITIAL CONDITIONS

Causality:
I object.

Physics:
on what grounds

Causality:
you're asking for authorization
before there was a "before."

Power Plant:
wrong jurisdiction?

Causality:
wrong temporal domain.
```

Then:

```text
Physics:
Spacetime?

Spacetime:
...

Physics:
Spacetime?

Spacetime:
zzz
```

`zzz` closes the chapter, not the book.

## Chapter 21: Hyprvisor remains the ending

The existing virtualization chapter remains deliberately light. Chapter 21 applies its host/guest distinction to the guest kernel and adds no second virtualization lecture.

The Larpintosh exchange remains:


The question hangs until the established final beat answers it:

```text
hyprvisor clears its throat.

```

There is no explanatory sentence, evidence note, colophon, or generated back-cover copy after it inside `manuscript.md`.

## Evidence architecture

Add or extend receipt families for:

- SoC and Application Processor terminology, using Apple public material where available;
- independent processor/security-domain boundaries already established for SEP;
- boot-stage handoff versus process parentage;
- OIK and AMFI public roles, with observations separated from public guarantees;
- shutdown and power-state language, avoiding claims about undocumented internal sequencing;
- Apple platform power-management concepts at the level required by the scene;
- general electrical-infrastructure claims from authoritative public sources only where the prose makes a factual claim.

Add dramatization identifiers for SoC dialogue, fictional `amfidd`, fictional `iBootd`, Five-Minute Kernel, infrastructure dialogue, metaphysical postscript, and Hyprvisor.

The reading copy should stay light. Receipts support load-bearing claims without turning the chapter voice into a lab report.

## Word allocation

The 30,000–35,000-word range is directional, not a quota.

- Distributed additions across Chapters 2, 5, 8, and 9: approximately 2,000–3,000 new words total.
- Chapter 18 expansion: approximately 1,000–1,500 new words.
- Chapter 19 expansion: approximately 700–1,100 new words.
- New Chapter 20: approximately 2,000–3,000 words.
- Chapter 21 receives only continuity edits.

If a section grows by restating an established boundary, cut it. Length is earned by a new mechanism, collision, consequence, or receipt.
