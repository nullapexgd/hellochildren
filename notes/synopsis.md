Your Mac is not run by one all-powerful piece of software. It is run by a dysfunctional bureaucracy.

*On Your Processor* is a field guide to the overlapping jurisdictions inside modern macOS and Apple silicon: Boot ROM and iBoot deciding what may run, XNU governing the kernel, launchd raising userspace, WindowServer owning the graphical world, and the Secure Enclave guarding a security domain of its own.

The expanded tour follows a request sideways and downward. APFS argues about which file exists. Virtual memory separates addresses from residency. MMU and DART enforce different maps. CPU caches negotiate shared reality. Drivers, firmware, controllers, networks, schedulers, and wake machinery all perform narrow jobs while somebody farther upstairs takes credit.

Through technical explanation, primary-source receipts, labeled reverse engineering, and increasingly hostile family dialogue, the book asks one question over and over: who is actually in charge?

It depends what you are trying to do. Inside a modern computer, authority has a jurisdiction.
